Feel it

It swells, splits, pours back together, all in the same second.

Here's the idea
No lead bird. No plan. No one can see the whole flock. Each starling tracks only about seven of its nearest neighbors, matching their speed and spacing, moment to moment. Out of nothing but that, a vast coordinated whole appears that no single bird intended. EMERGENCE: a big pattern grows upward out of small relationships. No headquarters. Name it
"Small is good, small is all. The large is a reflection of the small." adrienne maree brown, Emergent Strategy, 2017. She lowercases her name.

What does she mean by "the large is a reflection of the small"?

Here's the idea

adrienne maree brown is an organizer and writer. Her 2017 book Emergent Strategy makes one core move: stop borrowing your strategy from war and advertising (conquer, scale, dominate), and start borrowing it from living systems (adapt, connect, grow from the small). Much of it she draws from the science-fiction writer Octavia Butler, who imagined communities that survive collapse by bending instead of breaking. Her claim about the large reflecting the small is simple and hard to dodge. A big thing carries the character of its smallest parts. You cannot fake healthy soil at scale. You build it in one handful of compost, and the whole farm becomes a reflection of that. Movements work the same way, for better or worse.

Fractal

A shape that repeats at every scale. A fern where each tiny frond is the same shape as the whole fern. A coastline equally jagged from a plane or from a footpath. brown's version for people: how we are at the small scale is how we are at the large scale.

Think of a group you were part of that fell apart or turned toxic. Was the bad feeling already there in the small, in how two people talked to each other, long before it was ever big?

Move at the speed of trust

Trust cannot be rushed. Outrun it and the thing tears. A stranger asks you for a huge favor on day one, and you say no. The relationship is not there yet to carry the ask.

Critical connections over critical mass

You do not need a huge crowd. You need a few real, strong relationships that can actually bear weight. Build resilience by building relationships, not follower counts.

Which is stronger: ten thousand followers who like your posts and do nothing, or twelve people who will show up at 6am when you call?

A different way to grow

An ad campaign is one big loud push, then it stops when the money stops. This fungus grows the other way. What is different about how it grows, and why might a movement want to grow like this?

Here's the idea
Mycelium is the real organism. The mushroom you see is just the small fruit. It grows mostly invisible, relational, decentralized, and patient, a vast web of threads under the forest floor. Cut one thread and the network survives. There is no headquarters to shut down. Lasting movements grow like this. The visible part is small on purpose. The strength is underground.
A movement with no headquarters
Fayetteville, Arkansas, 2016. A woman named Jessica McClard built one small wooden box on a post outside a church, stocked it with food, and painted three words on it: take what you need, leave what you can. She called it a little free pantry. No nonprofit, no campaign, no staff. She built one good small thing and posted a photo of it. People copied it. Not her organization, because there was none. They copied the pattern. One pantry, then a hundred, then thousands, each built and stocked and watched over by whoever lives nearby, in whatever wood and paint they had.

Growing by copying a small pattern, with no central plan, gave this movement something a single big national campaign never could. Name it.
