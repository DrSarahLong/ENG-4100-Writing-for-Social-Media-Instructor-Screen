Goal of today

What you'll walk out with

Today we name three ideas that change how you read your own feed: communicative capitalism, the attention economy, and Jenny Odell's idea of refusing it in place. You'll test them on campaigns that went huge and changed nothing, and on small quiet ones that changed a lot. Thursday's module goes deeper; today we dig in together.

Feel it

Name one thing in the real world that changed because of something you posted.

A person, a dollar, a vote, a policy, a door that opened. Real and offline only. A like on the post does not count.

Name it

What's the difference between a message that wants a reply and a message that just wants to circulate?

Texting one friend "please water my plant" versus posting "please water my plant" to ten thousand strangers.

Here's the idea

In 2005, before the iPhone, the political theorist Jodi Dean named this. On networked platforms your political speech stops being a message, something a person receives and has to answer, and becomes a contribution to circulation, one more unit added to the flow, valued only for moving. The count is the product. She calls the result communicative capitalism. The passion that could have become action gets spent as posting instead. It feels like doing something. That feeling is the trap.

What runs out

Anyone can post forever, for free. Information is basically unlimited. So what is actually scarce?

Here's the idea
A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention. (Herbert Simon, 1971) Attention is the scarce resource, so it is the valuable one. The free app is not a charity. You are the product, and your attention is the raw material being sold to advertisers. That reframes the whole problem: what would it mean to take yours back?
The case · Kony 2012

In March 2012 an advocacy group released a 30-minute film urging the world to help capture a warlord. It hit about 100 million views in six days, the fastest-spreading video to that point in history. Then almost nothing followed. He was never captured. The real-world action fizzled. The organization fell apart within a year.

More reach than your cause will ever get, and it produced less than sixty phone calls would. What was it missing?

A way out?

If the answer isn't "log off forever and stop caring," what could refusing the attention economy actually look like?

Jenny Odell wrote a whole book called How to Do Nothing. She is not telling you to quit.

Here's the idea

Jenny Odell's "doing nothing" is two moves at once. Refuse to hand your attention to the machine that turns it into circulation. Then re-place that attention on something real: a place, a community, the actual people and living things in front of you. Refusal in place, not escape. Where you put your attention is where your life goes, and reclaimed attention is the ground everything real stands on. You cannot organize what you can't even notice. For a cause, that means aiming the same energy at things that produce a reply and a relationship that lasts past the scroll.

What it circulates

What does this move spread well? What kind of reach can it get?

Where it dead-ends

Who has to receive it or answer it right now? If the answer is nobody, that is the dead end.

Who could answer

Name one real person or office that could be made to respond. Where is the power this should land on?

Wire it to action

The one ask, channel, or captured contact that turns the reach into a reply. Redesign the move so something catches it.

Your cause · Field Notebook

Open your Field Notebook. Is your cause building circulation, or action?

Work these three on your own, then we'll hear a few. Most of it will land in "circulation," and that's the diagnosis, not a failure.

List five things your cause did to reach people in the last month. Beside each, write CIRCULATION (added to the flow, measured in likes and views) or ACTION (got a reply, met a person, changed a condition).
Pick one that's stuck in circulation. In one sentence: what would turn it into action? A reply expected, a concrete ask, a real person met.
Odell's move: name one channel or vanity metric to WITHDRAW your attention from, and one real, grounded place to RE-PLACE it (a relationship or condition you'll build instead).
