Early 1980s · a new plague

If the people in power will not even say the name of the thing killing you, what does that silence tell you?

A new disease was spreading through gay men, drug users, and other people the country was already happy to ignore. The President gave no major public speech about it until 1987, years in, after tens of thousands of Americans were already dead.

Why is reclaiming your oppressor's own symbol so powerful?

That pink triangle is borrowed. In the Nazi camps, gay men were forced to wear a downward-pointing pink triangle to mark them for death. These activists flipped it to point up.

Here's the idea

Design activism

Making your argument with an image, a color, or a symbol instead of a paragraph. The picture does the arguing.

Gran Fury

ACT UP's design cell, named for the Plymouth the NYPD drove. Rage and a police car in one name. They dressed protest in the look of advertising so it traveled the same rails everyone already scrolls.

Why SILENCE=DEATH works as design
  • black reads as mourning and a void
  • the pink triangle is a Nazi death-badge, reclaimed and flipped point-up
  • three words, huge contrast, no logo, no explanation
  • it does not argue, it makes you finish the equation yourself
  • it looks like an ad, so it travels like one
One image, basically no budget, still legible on a t-shirt forty years later.

The scaling problem

Small groups trust each other but can't reach anyone. Big crowds have reach, but strangers don't trust each other or move fast together.

Affinity group

Five to fifteen people who know and trust each other, plan their own piece of an action, and look after their own members. Many groups plug into one big demonstration.

Why is a crowd built from small bonded cells harder to stop than one giant leaderless mob?

Drama on purpose

They lay down and played dead while the cameras rolled. Why stage your own death for a photograph?

Fake tombstones, blood-spattered lab coats, hundreds of bodies still on the pavement. All of it built for one viewer.

Here's the idea
Drama as strategy
  • a zap is fast, surprise, one target, embarrass them today
  • an action is big, planned, staged for the camera
  • the die-in gives the news a picture it can't resist
  • the bonded cells make the crowd hard to behead: no single leader to arrest, trust lives in the group
FDA action, 1988. About 1,500 activists, roughly 180 arrests, on camera, on purpose. Within months the FDA changed its rules to release experimental drugs faster. The image did what a press release never could.
The AIDS Memorial Quilt (1987 on)
  • each panel is 3 by 6 feet, the size of a grave
  • one panel per person lost, sewn by the people who loved them
  • first laid on the National Mall in 1987: 1,920 panels
  • now the largest piece of community folk art on earth
Quilting is one of the oldest folk media there is, and it runs deep here in Appalachia: memorial quilts, and the quilting bee, where a community gathers to sew and to grieve together.
Two speeds, one silence

The die-in and the Quilt fought the same silence. What did the slow Quilt do that a fast zap never could, and what did the zap do that the Quilt couldn't?
