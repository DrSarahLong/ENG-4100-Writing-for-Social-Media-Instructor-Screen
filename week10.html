Name it

Being leaderless sounds free and democratic. Why would it be the exact thing that freezes a movement?

Here's the idea

The tactical freeze

A movement gets enormous fast, then finds it can't switch tactics, authorize anyone to negotiate, or accept a partial win. Zeynep Tufekci, Twitter and Tear Gas (2017).

Why it happens

The crowd scaled up overnight without ever building the muscle to decide together. So it defaults to the one move everyone recognizes. At Tahrir, that meant going back to the square. Tufekci's phrase: "Tahrir or bust."

Leaderless has a real upside. No single person can be arrested, bought off, or discredited to kill the movement. The cost shows up later, when the moment calls for a new move and there is no agreed way to choose one.

A harder case

A quarter-million people arrived and left in one day without incident. What did that flawless march signal to those in power, and why did it scare them more than the size of the crowd?

Here's the idea
The slow work built the crowd AND built the muscle:
  • the meetings taught the group how to decide together
  • the roles taught them who could speak for whom
  • the shared labor built trust that survives a bad day
Skip the slow work and you get the crowd without the muscle. A flawless march advertised a hidden machine: "we can coordinate a quarter-million people, so imagine what else we can coordinate." A hashtag can now produce a bigger crowd overnight and prove none of that, which is part of why sheer size means less than it used to.
Bring it home

We love the speed of the hashtag and we don't want the freeze. So what do we now have to do on purpose that used to happen by accident?

Think about what an old movement was forced to build before it could gather anyone, and what a phone now lets us skip.

The one demand

What specifically do you want? Something a person in power could actually say yes or no to, not "raise awareness."

Who decides

Name the two or three people allowed to speak for the group and the rule they use to say yes to an offer. Be concrete.

The floor

What survives Saturday, after the crowd goes home? A sign-up list, a group chat, a standing meeting. How do you reach these people again?
