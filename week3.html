A country of strangers.

In 1788, a few people changed a nation's mind with newspaper essays signed by a fake name. The same machinery runs a viral post today.
Debate this · in your pods

"Arguing under a fake name is cowardice. If you believe it, sign it."

Goal of today

What you'll walk out with

By the end of class you'll be able to take apart how any movement spreads an idea, using three tools: circulation (how a message travels and gets repeated), a public (the body of strangers a shared text calls into being), and the mask (the choice to sign your name, hide it, or share one). We'll pull them out of a 1788 newspaper fight, then aim them at your own cause. You'll go deeper Thursday; today we dig in together.

The problem

You have to change the mind of a whole state full of strangers.

It's 1787. No internet, no TV, no radio, no phones. Most of these people you will never meet. How do you reach them, and how do you get them to act together?

Here's the idea

Circulation

How a text moves from person to person and place to place, and how often it gets repeated. A message is only as strong as its ability to travel.

A public

A body of strangers brought into being by a shared text. Reading the same thing, and knowing others are too, turns scattered people into a public that can be moved.

Here's the part that flips it around. A public does not sit around waiting for a message. The message creates the public. Tell ten thousand strangers "you are called upon to decide," and let each one know the others are reading the same words, and a scattered crowd becomes one body with a shared job. A hashtag runs the exact same move. Strangers post the same words, see each other doing it, and a public appears that wasn't there the day before.

The first essay · New York, 1787
"AFTER an unequivocal experience of the inefficiency of the subsisting federal government, you are called upon to deliberate on a new Constitution for the United States of America… It has been frequently remarked that it seems to have been reserved to the people of this country, by their conduct and example, to decide the important question, whether societies of men are really capable or not of establishing good government from reflection and choice, or whether they are forever destined to depend for their political constitutions on accident and force."
Read it like a stranger

Four questions. Take them one at a time.

What is he literally arguing? Say it in plain modern English.
Who is "you" in this? What does the passage turn the reader into?
What is he making you feel? Point to the exact words doing it.
It's 1787. How does this physically get from his desk into thousands of heads?
Look at the byline

It's signed Publius. That's not a person.

How many people actually wrote it? And why would anyone hide their name on the most important argument of their life?

Here's the idea
Publius was one shared mask over three men: Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, and John Jay. Eighty-five essays, one fake name, borrowed from a founder of the Roman Republic. A name can argue before the first sentence does. Why wear one mask?
  • the argument stands on its own, not on who is talking
  • three men speaking as one voice looks like consensus and inevitability
  • safety and cover in a hot, dangerous political fight
  • borrowed credibility from a Roman republican hero
A shared mask amplified the message and shielded the people behind it. Both at once.
The other side wore a mask too

Publius did not write into silence. Weeks earlier, an Anti-Federalist signing himself Brutus (the Roman who helped kill a tyrant) was already in the same New York papers arguing the opposite: a republic this large will swallow liberty. Same medium, same "you decide" move, another borrowed mask. The Federalist essays were written partly to answer him.

So the mask was never a good-guy tool or a bad-guy tool. Anonymity shields a whistleblower and it shields a liar. The authorship choice is itself a move, with a real trade attached every time.

Bring it home

Where is Publius today? And when is a mask the right call, versus just hiding?

Think of a hashtag, an anonymous org account, a collective, a burner. What does the mask buy the message, and what does it cost?
